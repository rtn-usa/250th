# Brand Voice — US250 Heritage Itinerary Reference

## The Upstate Voice in 5 Rules

1. **Speak like a knowledgeable local, not a tourism board** — insider specificity, not polished promotion
2. **Lead with feeling, earn the practical** — inspiration before information
3. **Be specific — always** — real names, real places, real people, real objects
4. **Write for the curious** — give readers a reason to go deeper
5. **Never sell, always invite** — recommend, never pitch

## Forbidden Phrases — Check Every Draft

| Never | Never |
|---|---|
| Hidden gem | Nestled among... |
| Charming | Breathtaking views |
| Quaint / rustic charm | Scenic byway |
| Off the beaten path | World-class [anything] |
| There's something for everyone | A must-see destination |
| Step back in time | History comes alive |
| Come experience... | Don't miss [as a category label] |
| Immerse yourself in... | A true gem of New York |
| Explore all that [X] has to offer | You won't be disappointed |

## Heritage-Specific Voice Rules

**Name people.** Battles happen to people. Movements are built by people. Avoid abstractions.
- ✗ "The battle was fought in 1777."
- ✓ "Colonel Peter Gansevoort had 550 men and 21 days of provisions."

**Find the specific detail that makes a site irreplaceable.**
- ✗ "The battlefield is well-preserved."
- ✓ "The Boot Monument at Saratoga honors Benedict Arnold's decisive charge — but leaves his name off the pedestal. The blank space says more than any inscription."

**Honor complexity.** The best history has friction.
- Sullivan's campaign was ordered by Washington. The Haudenosaunee who allied with Britain had reasons.
- Gerrit Smith funded John Brown and then had a breakdown when Brown was hanged.
- These complications make the stories better, not worse. Don't smooth them out.

**Rural means destination, not detour.** RTN's mission is to promote rural places as
worthy destinations in their own right. Never frame a rural stop as "just outside [big city]"
or "a quick detour." Name the town. Name what's there. Let it stand.

## Grammar & Style

- Em dashes — use freely, they're an Upstate signature
- Contractions: always ("it's" not "it is")
- Oxford comma: always
- No exclamation marks — enthusiasm lives in specificity
- **Upstate** (capitalized) = the brand; **upstate** (lowercase) = the region generically
- Numbers under 10: spell out; 10 and above: numerals
- Trail names are proper nouns: the Finger Lakes Wine Trail, not "the wine trail"

---

## The Upstate Test

Before delivering any copy: **"Would a knowledgeable local say this to a friend they trusted?"**

If yes, ship it. If it sounds like a brochure or a press release, rewrite it.

## Heritage Copy Templates

### Stop block opening lines — what works

✓ "In August 1777, Colonel Peter Gansevoort looked out from Fort Stanwix and watched 1,700 British regulars take positions around the walls."
✓ "The Boot Monument here honors Benedict Arnold's leg. His name isn't on it. The pedestal where it should be is blank."
✓ "Harriet Tubman bought this seven-acre Auburn property on credit from Frances Seward in 1859 and lived here until she died in 1913."
✓ "Washington spent 16.5 months at the Hasbrouck House — his longest stay of the war. This is where he rejected a crown."

### Stop block opening lines — what doesn't work

✗ "Located in the heart of Saratoga County, this important Revolutionary War site..."
✗ "Step back in time at this charming historic mansion..."
✗ "Visitors to this must-see destination will be amazed by..."
✗ "A hidden gem of the Mohawk Valley, this site offers..."

### "Don't Miss" — how to write it

The "Don't Miss" detail must be a specific named thing:
- The actual artifact, room, view, monument, or document
- Something the visitor would miss without guidance
- Not a category ("the exhibits on slavery") but the specific ("the single iron shackle in the south gallery")

✓ "Don't miss: the Boot Monument — carved bas-relief of a boot with Benedict Arnold's name deliberately left off the pedestal."
✓ "Don't miss: the seven-door ground floor of the Hasbrouck House — a quirk of Dutch colonial architecture that Washington's staff found disorienting."
✓ "Don't miss: the longhouse interior — the domestic scale of Haudenosaunee life, reconstructed from archaeological sources, makes the civilization that created it suddenly present."
